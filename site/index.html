Four reasons Stillwater makes sense

1. The customers are already here

Stillwater is a young city, with a median age of just 23.9 and 49,269 residents. OSU enrolled 26,805 students across its Stillwater and Tulsa campuses in fall 2024, and nearly 5,200 freshmen started in Stillwater in fall 2025.

The growth keeps coming. The OSU System hit a record 37,411 students in fall 2026. That is a large, steady crowd that eats out often and stays up late.

2. There is a gap on the map

Waffle House already operates in Oklahoma. The nearest stores are in north Oklahoma City and around Tulsa, including Owasso and Catoosa. Stillwater sits right between both markets with nothing of its own.

Stillwater has other breakfast spots like IHOP, but nothing like a Waffle House counter that never closes. Since the brand already serves OKC and Tulsa, a Stillwater store fits right into a region it knows.

3. Built for 24/7 life and Oklahoma weather

Waffle House aims to stay open 24 hours a day, 365 days a year. It is so dependable that former FEMA administrator Craig Fugate created the Waffle House Index to measure how bad a disaster is. If even Waffle House closes, things are bad.

Stillwater sits in Tornado Alley. A restaurant famous for staying open through storms would serve the whole community, not just the late night crowd.

4. The price fits the market

The median household income in Stillwater is $43,700, which makes sense in a town full of students. Waffle House is known for cheap, filling meals, which is exactly what this market spends on. And a habit that starts freshman year can last all four years.
