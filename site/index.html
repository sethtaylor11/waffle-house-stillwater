The market research

Four reasons Stillwater makes sense

01

The customers are already here

Stillwater is a young city, with a median age of just 23.9 and 49,269 residents. OSU enrolled 26,805 students across its Stillwater and Tulsa campuses in fall 2024, and nearly 5,200 freshmen started in Stillwater in fall 2025.

The growth keeps coming. The OSU System hit a record 37,411 students in fall 2026. That is a large, steady crowd that eats out often and stays up late.

02

There is a gap on the map

Waffle House already operates in Oklahoma. The nearest stores are on West Memorial Road in north Oklahoma City and around Tulsa, including Owasso and Catoosa. Stillwater sits right between both markets with nothing of its own.

Stillwater has other breakfast options, like IHOP on North Main Street, but nothing like a Waffle House counter that never closes. Since the brand already serves OKC and Tulsa, a Stillwater store slots right into a region it knows.

Simplified map, not to scale

03

Built for 24/7 life and Oklahoma weather

Waffle House aims to stay open 24 hours a day, 365 days a year. It is so dependable that former FEMA administrator Craig Fugate created the Waffle House Index to measure how bad a disaster is. Green means a full menu, yellow means a limited menu, and red means even Waffle House had to close.

Stillwater sits in the heart of Tornado Alley. A restaurant famous for staying open through storms would serve the whole community, not just the late night crowd.

04

The price fits the market

The median household income in Stillwater is $43,700, which makes sense in a town full of students. Waffle House is known for affordable, filling meals, which is exactly what this market spends on. Students are also loyal, and a habit that starts freshman year can last all four years.

"If a Waffle House is closed because there's a disaster, it's bad."

Craig Fugate, former FEMA administrator
